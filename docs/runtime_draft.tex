\paragraph{Running time.}
\alon{Provisional: preview run, three runs per point; the full run replaces these numbers.}
We time \sys{}, \sysopt{}, \Bone{}, \Btwo{}, \BF{}, \SingleStage{} and two exhaustive searches over the candidates of every view, one with the pruning of \Btwo{} and one without, on every pair, one parameter varied at a time (Figure~\ref{fig:scalability}; Figure~\ref{fig:attrs}, top, for $|\Attrs|$).
All methods of a run are timed on one machine, one after the other; we compare methods within runs.
At the defaults, \sys{} takes $0.12$--$0.47$\,s on Adult, COMPAS, Law Students and MEPS, $3.5$\,s on Tax and $24$\,s on ACSIncome ($1.7$ million tuples), and \sysopt{} is $5.1$--$11.4\times$ faster per pair (median $8.4\times$) and releases the same explanations in all $96$ runs compared.
\BF{} and \SingleStage{} take about a third of the time of \sys{} ($0.28$--$0.43$ per pair): they compute no Use and build the histograms of every view instead, while the Use pass over every view is $94$--$99\%$ of the time of \sys{}.
\Btwo{} is exponential in $k$: $7$--$57$\,s per pair at $k=6$, $2.3$--$15.5$ minutes at $k=7$, and over the limit from $k=8$ on every dataset.
The exhaustive search over all candidates enumerates as many sets, since the pruning keeps the $k$ best candidates of every attribute in both, and takes the same time wherever the enumeration dominates (median ratio $0.99$); at the defaults it is faster than \Btwo{} on ACSIncome ($10$ against $25$\,s) because it computes no Use.
Without the pruning, the search finishes at $k \le 2$ ($10$\,s per pair at $k=2$, at most $36$\,s); from $k=3$ it exceeds the limit, and its time extrapolated from the rate of its first block is about eight hours at $k=3$ and $5{,}000$ years at $k=5$ (median over the pairs; $52$ years on COMPAS, $10^5$ on Tax).
At $k=7$, \Btwo{} takes about $27$ minutes at $11$ attributes, at the limit ($20$ of its $69$ runs exceed it), and exceeds it from $12$.
From a tenth of the views to all of them, the time of \sys{}, \BF{} and \SingleStage{} grows $6.5$--$11\times$ (median $8.5\times$) and that of \sysopt{} at most $2.2\times$ (median $1.3\times$); \sysopt{} grows instead with $\tau$ ($3.5\times$ from $\tau=15$ to $\tau=|\mathcal{P}|$, against $1.3\times$ for \sys{}), since it builds the histograms of every retained view.
With a tenth of the views \sysopt{} saves nothing (on $8$ of the $36$ pairs it is up to $16\%$ slower than \sys{}).
